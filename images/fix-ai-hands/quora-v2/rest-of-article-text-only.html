Always look at the whole picture afterward

A nice close-up of the hand can hide a ruined face or outfit, so I compare the full file I downloaded with the original, checking:

  • Anatomy and gesture: the right number of fingers, joints that bend sensibly, and the pose you wanted.
  • Hand-object contact: fingers hold the object without blending into it, and the object still has its original shape.
  • Wrist and sleeve: a clean line between them with no smudging.
  • Identity and style: the face, hair and line thickness match everything else.
  • Lighting and shadows: the skin colour and the shadows where things touch suit the scene.
  • Dimensions: keep track of the size you downloaded. Compare details at the same marked scale, because a bigger file can look sharper without the repair actually being better.

Hand Fix and inpainting gave me files the same size as the originals, and I didn't notice any visible changes away from the hand. Reference Pro gave me bigger files and changed things all over the image. If keeping your character looking the same is what you care about, that counts for more than sharpness does.

What to do if your first attempt goes wrong

My one real failure was inpainting the mug. I'd painted over the entire hand, part of the handle included, and asked for the fingers to hold the handle firmly. Since the handle was within the mask, it was redrawn along with the hand. What I got was a fist squeezing a dented mug that had lost its handle completely.

To fix it, I redid the mask so it only covered the hand, with the handle left outside, and added a sentence to the prompt asking it to leave the mug's shape and handle exactly as they were. Everything else stayed the same (feathering 8, strength 0.75), and it cost 1,500 credits again. This time the mug held its shape and the handle came back, though the fingers aren't curled around it. The outer edge of the hand and the spot where it meets the sleeve look a bit scratchy, and the mug looks smoother than it did originally.

And when should you give up? If one mask has failed twice and each new attempt breaks something else, another generation probably won't save it. By then, it's often faster to paint the bad finger yourself in your own editing app than to keep spending credits.

Other questions people ask

Can an automatic hand fixer get rid of extra fingers? Yes, in my test. Fix hands removed the sixth finger in one go. It's still worth checking the fingertips and outlines afterward.

What do I write in an inpainting prompt for a hand? Describe the gesture, say how many fingers, and list anything that has to stay where it is, like the mug handle or the sleeve. Something vague like "make this a normal finger" was enough for a tiny extra finger sitting on its own, but not when there's an object right next to the hand.

Why does fixing the hand change the sleeve or the object it's holding? Anything in or close to your selection might get redrawn, and feathering blurs the edges together. Tools that work on the whole image, such as Reference Pro, redraw all of it.

Will upscaling fix wrong hand anatomy? No. An upscaler adds detail to what's already in the picture, so an extra finger just turns into a sharper extra finger. I didn't include an upscaler in this test.
